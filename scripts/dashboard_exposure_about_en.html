What this dashboard measures

It turns Chinese official finance amounts into "dependence". Six default indicators: (1) public-sector debt commitments 2020–23 under AidData's ODA+OOF main measure as a share of GDP; (2) public and publicly guaranteed (PPG) external debt owed to China in 2023 as a share of GDP; (3) debt to China as a share of all PPG external debt; (4) Chinese commitments 2020–23 relative to gross ODA disbursements from all official donors (including multilaterals) over the same period (how few alternatives there are); (5) rescue loans (Rescue: currency-swap drawdowns, BoP loans, budget support) as a share of GDP; (6) use of IMF credit in 2023 as a share of GDP (a proxy for fiscal stress / IMF programmes). Optional additions: AidData private debt as a share of GDP (the channel of Chinese firms' local interests), CGIT investment + construction as a share of GDP, CODF commitments as a share of GDP.

The indicators form concepts: Debt (debt to China as a share of GDP and of external debt: already locked in), Dependence (public-sector commitments as a share of GDP, rescue lending as a share of GDP, Chinese commitments / official ODA, IMF credit as a share of GDP, optional CODF: seeking help now, no alternative sources, fiscal stress) and Firms (AidData private debt as a share of GDP, CGIT investment + construction as a share of GDP: the local presence of Chinese firms). All nine indicators are included by default (since 2026-11-09). For each concept the sub-score is the mean percentile of the selected indicators; the total is the mean of the available sub-scores. Concepts are weighted equally and no single indicator is weighted. Percentiles are computed among all countries with a value for that indicator, so scores change with the selected indicators; "minimum number of indicators with values" stops small countries with only one or two data points from ranking high on a single indicator.

How to read it

  • This ranks exposure; it does not predict positions on Taiwan. A high score only means the country would pay a high price if Beijing applied pressure. Many high-scoring countries (Angola, Djibouti, Republic of the Congo) are already fully in Beijing's camp, with no observable change at the margin.
  • Taiwan's current diplomatic allies have no official financial ties with China, so their indicators are zero or missing and they do not appear in the table; the leverage over them is "the price Beijing offers", which has to be estimated separately from cases before and after a switch (Solomon Islands, Kiribati, Nauru, Honduras).

Data limitations

  • Debt stocks owed to China come from World Bank IDS, which covers only 134 low- and middle-income reporting countries; high-income countries (including Europe and the Gulf) have no value for this indicator.
  • OECD figures are disbursements while AidData's are commitments; the ratio is for relative comparison only.
  • The IMF MONA programme database refuses automated downloads, so IDS use of IMF credit (including SDR-related items) is used as a proxy.
  • Sovereign credit ratings are not included (not public).
  • AidData ends in 2023; CODF and CLA in 2024; CGIT runs to the first half of 2026.
